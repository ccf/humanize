# Research review: academic work beyond StoryScope (2026-09-14)

Status: decision input, not a plan. Nothing here is implemented yet.

## How this was produced

A 72-agent workflow on 2026-09-14: ten parallel literature sweeps (lexical
fingerprints, grammar/register, human perception, creative writing,
homogenization, interpretable detection features, discourse/pragmatics,
bias/fairness, editing practice, 2025–2026 work) found 80 distinct papers;
18 with relevance ≥ 2 were deep-read (each paper fetched and checked against
its own text); every proposed change was then judged by two independent
adversarial lenses — accuracy (does the paper say this?) and
applicability/risk (already covered? generalizes past the paper's genre?
evasion-oriented? fairness cost?). 206 proposals → 93 survived both lenses →
synthesized per component → assembled below. A completeness critic then
reviewed the memo; its report is appended and its disputes stand as recorded.

Read the memo with the critic's disputes in hand. Where they disagree, the
critic is usually right about scope (do not add five reference files and six
principles; drop `--halves` and `adverbs.ly_rate`; ship nominalization as
visible hits only).

# Research memo: other academic work worth leveraging in `humanize`

## 1. Bottom line

Eighteen studies were reviewed and ten of them change the plugin; the other eight are already covered, unverifiable, or measure something other than human-vs-AI difference. Beyond StoryScope the literature adds four things the plugin has no form of today — a grammar/register layer (nominalization, participial modifiers, informational density, contraction level), a repetition-and-lexical-diversity family, localization (tells cluster in passages, not evenly over a document), and a reader-side result showing that most of what we measure is invisible to readers — plus one live correctness bug in `surface_scan.py`. What it does not add is a better base-rate corpus: StoryScope remains the only large-n measurement of 2026 models, while nearly every new number comes from GPT-3.5/GPT-4-era systems on news leads, student essays, biomedical abstracts and literary fiction, so directions transfer and magnitudes do not. Nor does it settle the conflicts it creates — word length, hedging and sentence fragments all now have evidence pointing both ways — so the recommendation is an adjudication rule, not a winner. The single largest contribution is negative: four converging studies show the measured "AI profile" also describes competent L2 English, translated text, plain-language and wire-service house style, and controlled genres where reuse is a correctness requirement, which is the fairness rail `principles.md` currently lacks.

## 4. Cautions the plugin should encode

**Fairness (non-native, formal and plain-language writers).** Every distributional metric added here — nominalization, mean word length, informational density, prepositional phrases, agentless passives, attributive adjectives, low lexical diversity, repeated phrasing, low contraction rate — is simultaneously the signature of academic, technical, legal and professional register, of English as taught to second-language writers, and of controlled genres where reuse is a correctness requirement. Liang et al. (2024) name the non-native-speaker population as an explicit confound on their own estimates; Shaib et al. (2024) found human Cochrane references *more* templated per token than 8 of 9 models; Rudnicka & Juzek (2026) note that formal, legal and academic registers avoid contractions by convention and many L2 writers are taught to. Encode this as principles row 1 plus the SKILL step-1 per-check gate, and state plainly that every human baseline in the evidence base comes from one population (StoryScope: amateur fiction; Herbold: non-native high-school writers; Jakesch: short self-presentation bios) whose own Limitations sections decline to generalize.

**Corpus age (GPT-3.5/4-era findings).** The survey's own conclusion warns that "the majority of the findings reported in this survey could apply primarily to GPT-3.5" (25 of 44 studies). Only StoryScope measures 2026 models. Three concrete expirations are already documented: MTLD reverses direction between ChatGPT-3.5 (75.68) and GPT-4 (108.91) against a human 95.72; lexical diversity is higher in human text than GPT-3.5 but *lower* than GPT-4 (Herbold); `delves` ran ×39.5 above trend at its mid-2024 peak and ×8.0 a year later. The operative rule (principles row 5): prefer recency for capability-dependent features, replication for stable ones; take directions from old corpora and never magnitudes; and do not add a type-token or vocabulary-repetition *check* at all, only a reported number.

**Genre limits.** Each source is bounded by a narrow corpus and several authors say so explicitly. LAMP is 80% literary fiction and its Limitations state the findings are "potentially less applicable to other genres like technical writing, journalism, or scientific writing." Muñoz-Ortiz is English NYT lead paragraphs capped at 200 tokens, with an acknowledged asymmetric prompt (humans wrote from the full article; models saw a headline plus three words). Herbold is 90 argumentative student essays of ~250–340 words. Kobak is ~250-word biomedical abstracts measured as document-presence, not per-1k density — its numbers must never be substituted into the plugin's existing thresholds. Sandler is crowdworker emotional-support dialogue with a 58-vs-300-word length gap. Every new reference entry therefore needs a `Genre note:` line, and nothing in this batch is validated for non-English prose (the survey: English in 40 of 44 studies).

**Detector-evasion drift.** Four of these papers are detection or attribution work, and their material converts into an evasion tool if shipped carelessly: Liang et al.'s Supplementary Tables 2–3 are literally a detector's ranked feature list; Sun et al.'s transformation experiments (paraphrase, translate, summarize) are attacks on an attribution classifier, with a 58.1% summarization result that reads as a recipe; Venkatraman et al.'s cross-segment variation is presented as a detector enhancement. The mitigations are structural, not cosmetic: keep the "never claim undetectable" ban, add principles row 4 so the *optimization* is forbidden and not only the *claim*, frame `--halves` as register consistency rather than seam-hunting, forbid the audit table from labelling any paragraph as model-written, and never ship a ranked detector feature list as a "remove these words" checklist. Jakesch's result gives the reason rather than a rule: text selected against reader heuristics was rated human 65.7% of the time — *above* real human writing at 51.7% — so optimizing for perceived humanity is a different and worse objective than writing well.

**Temporal drift of wordlists.** The AI wordlist is an RLHF-era artifact with a measurable shelf life. Rudnicka & Juzek's PCA over prompt-matched 2024 and 2026 corpora separates model generations on PC1 (37.6% of variance), and Kobak's own July 2025 update shows the flagship marker decaying by a factor of five within a year. Two failure modes follow, in opposite directions: under-firing on current model output, and over-firing on human formal prose that has absorbed the same vocabulary (Kobak explicitly cannot separate direct LLM use from humans absorbing LLM-preferred words). Encode the date stamp (surface-tells row 2), keep the new `FICTION_PHRASES` out of `AI_WORDLIST` so the published per-1k rule of thumb stays calibrated on the vocabulary it was measured on, and comment the most decay-prone entries as such.

# Completeness critic

## Gaps

- **LLM-as-judge self-preference never searched.** Drafting mode and step 6 both have Claude judge its own prose; models score their own generations higher in proportion to how well they recognize them (Panickssery 2024). *Next step:* make step 6 a re-scan of `surface_scan` numbers plus quoted spans only — ban "does this read human to me?" as the verification criterion, and say why in principles #5.
- **HCI writing-assistant corpora (CHI/UIST/IUI) beyond LAMP and Padmakumar.** CoAuthor has 1,445 sessions with per-span human/model provenance — exactly the held-out data the `--by-paragraph` localization work needs and does not have. *Next step:* before shipping hotspots, build 6–8 fixtures from mixed-provenance paragraphs and require hotspot detection to separate them.
- **The memo proposes ~60 changes and zero evaluation.** `tests/fixtures/` holds four files; every new metric arrives unvalidated on the plugin's own corpus. *Next step:* gate each new scanner block on "separates the AI from the human fixture", and expand fixtures first — this is the cheapest way to kill half the marginal rows.
- **Sentence-length *sequence* variance is unmeasured and is the cheapest thing left.** `cv`, tails and `p90` are all order-free; principle 7 ("vary sentence length") still has no metric. *Next step:* add `lag1_mean_abs_delta` and `longest_flat_run` (sentences within ±3 words) — ~10 lines, stdlib, and it catches the metronome that CV passes.
- **Non-English scope stated in the cautions, acted on nowhere.** *Next step:* one README/SKILL line scoping every base rate, the wordlist and all grammar ratios to English prose, plus a refusal to quote numbers on translated text.
- **Fiction-side quality evidence missing.** Herbold's "AI essays rated higher" is the sole source for "a tell is not a defect", but TTCW finds LLM stories pass 3–10x fewer creativity tests than professionals'. *Next step:* cite both and scope the principle by genre.
- **Journalism/copyediting practice research not searched** (style-guide and wire-desk edit taxonomies, distinct from LAMP's MFA annotators). *Next step:* low priority, only if the step-3 line-level prompt lands.

## Disputed recommendations

- **`--halves` (scanner #12, SKILL #4).** Relabelling it "register consistency" does not change the operation: locating machine-written spans is the classifier the non-goals forbid, and the memo's own guardrail concedes the source measured sub-sentence spans. Keep `--by-paragraph`, drop `--halves`.
- **`adverbs.ly_rate` (#16).** The memo states the paper never measures adverb rate and both regex and stoplist are broken as specified. "Ship last" should be "drop".
- **Five new reference files + six new principles.** SKILL.md is capped ~150 lines by invariant and loads `principles.md` every invocation; 7→13 principles and 5→9 reference files is itself a convergence failure. Fold `reader-heuristics` into principle 5, `survey-tells` into `Replication:` lines on existing entries, `expository-tells` into surface-tells.
- **Nominalization (top rec #10, scanner #7).** Regex suffix counting with no POS fires on every deadjectival `-ity`/`-ness`; the memo calls it the riskiest item and still ranks it top-ten. Ship hits-only (visible strings, no rate, no threshold) or not at all.
- **`FICTION_PHRASES` (#15).** "air was thick", "hung in the air" are cliché imagery, which `style-tells.md` already rates ("fresh" imagery, figurative density) — check for a second home before adding one.

## Additional papers

- **LLM Evaluators Recognize and Favor Their Own Generations** (2024) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13076 — self-preference bias scales with self-recognition; undercuts any step where Claude verifies its own rewrite by judgement rather than by scan.
- **Art or Artifice? Large Language Models and the False Promise of Creativity** (2023, CHI 2024) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14556 — TTCW, 10 expert writers on 48 stories: LLM stories pass 3–10x fewer creativity tests, and no LLM correlates with expert assessment.
- **Generative artificial intelligence enhances creativity but reduces the diversity of novel content** (Doshi & Hauser, 2023) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00506 — second causal homogenization result: AI-assisted stories rated better individually yet more similar to each other.
- **CoAuthor: Designing a Human-AI Collaborative Writing Dataset** (Lee, Liang, Yang, 2022) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.06796 — 63 writers × GPT-3 over 1,445 sessions with span-level provenance; the calibration corpus for localization.
